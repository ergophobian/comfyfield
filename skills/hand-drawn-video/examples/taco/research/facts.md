# Facts (each one on screen, with its source; checked 2026-10-29)

- A pineapple plant takes about 18–24 months from planting a crown to a ripe fruit. (University of Hawaiʻi CTAHR,
  "Pineapple" crop profile; FAO pineapple production guides.) On screen: "~2 years", "two years to grow".
- Pigs reach market weight at about 6 months. (USDA ERS hog production overview; Pork Checkoff "Life cycle of a market pig".)
- Jalisco is one of Mexico's largest pork-producing states. (SIAP / SADER Mexico livestock statistics.)
- Field corn for masa is harvested dry, about 4–5 months after sowing, after drying on the stalk. (CIMMYT maize guides.)
- Veracruz grows most of Mexico's pineapple; Sinaloa leads white-corn production; Michoacán is a leading lime
  (limón) state; Jalisco leads pork. (SIAP, Atlas Agroalimentario.) The map's four origins.
- Nixtamalization: dried corn cooked and steeped (often overnight) in water with calcium hydroxide ("cal",
  limewater), then ground into masa. (Standard food-science description; e.g. Tortilla Industry Association.)
- Al pastor: pork marinated in dried chiles and achiote, stacked on a vertical spit (trompo), topped with
  pineapple, shaved to order. (Widely documented; e.g. Mexico City food histories.)
- "Three bites", "8:02 PM", "Four states. One street corner." are story, not claims.
