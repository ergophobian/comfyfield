# Hand-drawn video

A film made this way is a web page that paints every frame on a canvas: warm paper, ink lines that wobble and
swell like a pen, flat colour with soft shade and grain, captions that type themselves. Its music is code too —
instruments synthesised from scratch, a score written on the film's own clock, so every cut and every key moment
can be answered by the sound. `engine/render.mjs` drives a headless Chrome/Edge to draw exact frames and ffmpeg
to encode them; nothing is generated by an image or video model.

Two loops make it good, and in both **whoever made it never grades it**:

- **The visual loop** — a fresh sound-off viewer says what each frame shows; a fresh judge scores the film against
  the criteria, writes a fix list and a keep list; the builder fixes; up to 3 rounds. [reference/visual-loop.md](reference/visual-loop.md)
- **The BGM loop** — a fresh music judge grades the soundtrack from what can be measured and read (the score, a
  piano roll with the film's cues on it, the spectrogram, loudness, sync measured on the real sound, and a listen
  where a model can); the composer revises; 2 rounds. [reference/bgm-loop.md](reference/bgm-loop.md)

Run the whole flow without stopping for approval. Where something is not said, take the default below, carry
on, and say at the end which defaults you used.

## Setup (once)

- Node 18+, ffmpeg on PATH (or `FFMPEG=`), Chrome / Edge / Chromium installed (or `CHROME_PATH=`).
- `npm install` in `engine/` (puppeteer-core, no browser download).
- Optional, for studying a reference's music and measuring yours: Python with `librosa`; better with
  `beat_this`, `demucs` and `basic-pitch` (`engine/tools/analyze_audio.py` uses what it finds and says what it skipped).
- If your harness can run agents in parallel or in the background, use it for the judges (always fresh ones)
  and, on a long film, for scene builders. Without that, run each judge as a separate session or a clean
  context that reads only its prompt and its evidence.

All commands: `node <skill>/engine/render.mjs <command> <project> …` — `render`, `stills`, `sheet`, `strip`,
`review` (a judging round's whole evidence pack), `music` (the soundtrack's report), `frames`, `info` (the top of
the file lists their flags).

## The flow

### 1. Read the ask
What it is about, who watches, and any reference. Defaults: 1080x1080 for a feed, 1080x1920 for phones/shorts
when they say TikTok/Reels/Shorts; the reference's length, else 30–40 s; 30 fps; music only, captions carry
the words (a narration only when given or asked). Brand marks only when they name them — then exactly theirs.

### 2. Study the reference (if there is one)
Frames: `ffmpeg -i ref.mp4 -vf "fps=1/2,scale=360:-1,tile=4x2" ref-%02d.png` and look at every sheet. Its music:
`python engine/tools/analyze_audio.py ref.mp4 --stems --out research/ref-audio`. Write `style.md` — palette (hex),
type and how words arrive, the two or three kinds of shot, how scenes change, pacing (seconds a scene), what
moves — and `research/music-study.md` (what the music does, measured). **Take the grammar, never the
content**: new subject, new places, new lines, new music (another key, metre and instrument set).
No reference: write `style.md` from [reference/craft.md](reference/craft.md) and choose.

### 3. Write the story — `story.md`
One line: the idea. Then every scene: its caption words verbatim (short, concrete, one idea each), what the
picture must make the viewer get, its one key moment. Every fact true and sourced (a line per fact in
`research/facts.md`). The first frame is a picture worth stopping for; the end is a payoff, not a fade.

### 4. Plan on the music's grid
Choose tempo and metre first (they are yours), so every bar has a known length. Scenes are whole bars; key
moments sit on beats or eighths; every cut is on a bar line. Write the timeline into the project's shared file
(see the taco example: `T`, `bar()`), and register each key moment as a cue — `K.cue(t, 'scene: what happens',
'hit')`. The cue list is what the music answers and what both judges check.

### 5. Build — with a look-and-fix loop on every scene
`node engine/new-project.mjs <dir>` copies the page and a fresh kit. Put the palette, caption helper, timeline and
shared drawings in one shared file; one file per scene. Kit and techniques: [reference/craft.md](reference/craft.md).
While building, look at your own pictures constantly — this inner loop is the builder's, not a grade:
`sheet --from a --to b --every 0.25` (the scene), `stills --times …` (detail), `strip --at T` (12 frames around the
fastest moment: pops, collisions, jumps). Fix and look again until each scene would sit beside an
illustrator's work. Several builders in parallel: give each the same written brief —
[reference/builder-brief.md](reference/builder-brief.md) — and give each scene to exactly one of them.

### 6. Compose — `score.js`
Write the music brief first (`music.md`): its signature, the clichés it avoids, the instruments drawn from the
subject's own world, the form mapped to the story's sections, how the cues are answered. Then the score, which
reads `K.film.cues` so sync is built in. `music --dir out/music-r0` and read your own report before any judge.
Everything about composing: [reference/bgm-loop.md](reference/bgm-loop.md).

### 7. Render the draft
`render <project> --out out/draft-r1.mp4 --workers 4`. Seconds per second of film on a normal PC; render in
the background where you can.

### 8. The visual loop (≤ 3 rounds; pass = every criterion ≥ 8)
`render.mjs review <project> --film out/draft-rN.mp4 --round N` makes the evidence pack; then a fresh sound-off
viewer, a fresh judge, fix only the fix list, re-render, next round with a new judge that first marks the last fixes. After round 3's fixes the film ships. [reference/visual-loop.md](reference/visual-loop.md)

### 9. The BGM loop (2 rounds; pass = every criterion ≥ 8)
Music report + measured analysis + piano roll + **a blind listen by two listeners** (any model that hears video
sound — the mix wrapped in a black clip) → fresh BGM judge → composer revises → round 2 with a new judge → the
round-2 fixes ship, after one more two-listener check. Listeners name what they hear by its library cliché; that
list is the most useful thing in the loop (what they named, in `reference/bgm-loop.md`). Run it beside the visual loop; a picture fix that moves a cue moves its sound with it,
since the score reads the cues. [reference/bgm-loop.md](reference/bgm-loop.md)

### 10. Final and hand-over
Final render; look at a whole-film sheet one more time (`sheet --every 1`) and a strip at every cut; play the
mp4's sound once through `analyze_audio.py` with `--cues` to confirm the sync. Deliver one mp4 where they
asked, named for the film. Say what was made, the defaults you chose, the loop results (each round's scores),
and anything you could not verify.

## What keeps it good

- **Measure before anyone looks**: the judges read numbers where numbers exist (text size, loudness, sync),
  and spend their eyes on what only eyes can judge.
- **Fresh eyes every round.** A judge that saw the last version is anchored on it; a builder never grades.
- **Fix lists lift scores, they don't redesign.** Only what lifts a sub-8 score, plus real errors; a keep list
  protects what works. "Nice to have" is never acted on in the loop.
- **No house look.** The kit gives a hand, not a style: each film's palette, type, shots and music are its own,
  taken from its subject and its reference.
- **Honest**: a skipped step is said; a fact not verified is marked; a judge's failing score is reported, not
  smoothed over.

## Files

- `engine/render.mjs` — frames, sheets, strips, the render, the music report, footage → frames
- `engine/new-project.mjs` — a new project from `engine/template/` + `engine/kit/`
- `engine/kit/` — `core.js` (time, springs, noise), `draw.js` (paper, ink, shapes, shade, hatching),
  `type.js` (typed captions, labels), `film.js` (scenes, transitions, cues, footage), `music.js` (synth, score,
  summary, piano roll), `player.js` (the page: preview player and the render hooks)
- `engine/tools/analyze_audio.py` — measure a reference's music or your own
- `reference/` — `craft.md`, `visual-loop.md`, `bgm-loop.md`, `builder-brief.md`
- `examples/taco/` — a finished film made with this skill: "How this taco was made."
